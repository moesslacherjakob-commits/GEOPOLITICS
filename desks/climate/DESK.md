## Where to look (last 24 h)
Story selection and facts come from newspapers, wire services and TV broadcasters:
- **Wire / broadcasters:** Reuters (Environment, World), AP (Climate, Weather), BBC (Weather, Climate, Science & Environment), CNN Weather, CBS News, ABC News, NBC News, DW Environment, Al Jazeera (Climate Crisis), France 24
- **Newspapers:** The Guardian (Environment), The New York Times (Climate), The Washington Post (Weather / Climate)
- **Data and official statements (to support, not to pick the story):** NOAA / National Hurricane Center (Atlantic and East Pacific advisories, track positions), JMA and JTWC (West Pacific typhoons), WMO, Copernicus Climate Change Service (monthly bulletins, early each month), national weather services (Met Office, IMD, PAGASA …), World Weather Attribution (rapid attribution studies)

Search queries with today's date, e.g. "hurricane <Month D, YYYY>", "typhoon <date>", "floods <date>", "heatwave record <date>", "wildfire <date>", "Copernicus <month> <year> temperature".

## How to pick
Score candidates on: number of major outlets leading with it · a genuinely new development (landfall, record, new death toll, new report) · global significance (people affected, records, climate signal) · can it be shown on a map in about a minute.
Typical winners: a tropical cyclone making landfall or breaking a record · major floods, heatwaves, droughts or wildfires · a new global temperature record or monthly Copernicus/WMO bulletin · a big attribution study · major climate-policy news only when it is the most-covered climate story of the day.
Avoid: purely local weather, forecasts for the coming days as the main story, unverified viral clips.

## Desk rules
- Report what happened and what officials and scientists said. **No own forecasts** and no safety instructions — for ongoing severe weather the disclaimer already points to local authorities; you may say "authorities have ordered evacuations".
- Casualty and damage numbers: always attributed, latest figure, with the time ("as of Saturday morning").
- Records: say who keeps the record (NOAA, WMO, Copernicus, national service) and since when records go back.
- Attribution claims ("made X times more likely") only from a named study (e.g. World Weather Attribution, Met Office) and named as such.
- Climate context (El Niño, warming oceans) only as stable background or attributed to scientists — no speculation linking a single event to climate change beyond what a named source says.
- Storm tracks and rain/heat areas drawn from reported positions are **approximate** — label them on screen ("TRACK APPROXIMATE", "ILLUSTRATIVE").

## Visual grammar
- **Hook:** `globe` zooming to the region, `chip` with the event name, `headline` (2 lines, second in `accent`).
- **Map** (main scene), pick what fits the story:
  - tropical cyclone → `track` with `steps` following the narration (formation → peak → landfall), `cat` per point, `name` + `sub` on the landfall point, `forecast` from the first future point; NHC/JMA positions or reported distances ("245 miles south of Pensacola") give the coordinates
  - floods / rain → `field` palette `rain`, `clip: "land"`, plus `marker`/`tag` for the worst-hit places
  - heatwave / drought → `field` palette `heat` or `drought`, `tint` for affected countries, `column` for record temperatures
  - wildfire → `field` palette `fire`, `marker style x` for evacuated towns
  - global records / ocean heat / El Niño → wide camera (`dist` 120–160), `field` palette `heat` over the ocean area, `tag`s for the regions
- **Overlays:** `counter` (people without power, rain totals, deaths — always with `source`), `facts` (record vs. normal), `bars` (this year vs. previous records), `stamp` for the record moment.
- **End:** globe on the region, two punchlines, sources line incl. "approximate" note.
- Section names like THE RECORD · THE LANDFALL · THE REASON / THE DAMAGE · THE TOLL · THE TREND. Vary colours, camera paths and overlays day to day.
