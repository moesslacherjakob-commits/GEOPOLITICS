## Where to look (last 24 h)
Story selection and facts come from newspapers, wire services and TV broadcasters:
- **Outlets:** Reuters (Science, Technology), AP (Science, Technology), BBC (Science & Environment, Technology), The Guardian (Science, Technology), The New York Times (Science, Technology), CNN (Science, Business/Tech), CBS/ABC/NBC News, DW Science, Al Jazeera (Science & Technology), Financial Times / Bloomberg / CNBC for tech business
- **Primary sources (to support and link, not to pick the story):** the original paper (Nature, Science, The Lancet, NEJM, Cell, arXiv for preprints), NASA/ESA/JAXA/ISRO mission pages, nobelprize.org, company announcements (as the company's claim), regulators (EU Commission, FTC, FCC, FDA)

Search queries with today's date, e.g. "science news <Month D, YYYY>", "study Nature <date>", "NASA <date>", "launch <date>", "AI <date> Reuters", "tech antitrust <date>".

## How to pick
The most-covered science or technology story of the last 24 h. Score on: number of major outlets covering it · genuinely new (a result, launch, decision, record) · global significance · can it be explained visually (mechanism, scale, timeline, map) in about a minute.
Typical winners: a major study in a top journal · space missions (launches, landings, discoveries) · Nobel and other major prizes · AI and chip breakthroughs or regulation · big-tech antitrust rulings and deals · medical breakthroughs (approved, not hyped) · energy technology (batteries, fusion milestones) · major cyber incidents.
Rotation: not more than two tech stories or two science stories in a row (check `log.md`). Avoid: product launch hype, gadget reviews, unverified "breakthrough" press releases, single-outlet exclusives.

## Desk rules
- **Peer review:** say "a study published in Nature" for peer-reviewed work; preprints are "not yet peer-reviewed". Early results, animal or lab studies are named as such.
- **Company claims are claims:** "OpenAI says…", "SpaceX plans…". Benchmarks and performance numbers only with who measured them.
- **No medical advice** and no "cure" language; the desk disclaimer adds "Nothing here is medical advice" automatically.
- Explain the mechanism in plain words; stable background facts (how neutrinos are detected, what a qubit is) are fine and can be labelled with the institution in the source line.
- Numbers exactly as sourced; when sources differ, use the more authoritative/recent one or leave it out.

## Visual grammar
- **Hook:** `globe` on the place that matters (launch site, lab, observatory, regulator) with `chip` and `headline` — or start straight with a `scale`/`counter` when the hook is a number.
- **Fact blocks:** pick per story —
  - `flow` (alias of `chain`) for "how it works" (3–4 steps, no `dir` arrows)
  - `timeline` for missions, research histories, product/regulation milestones (≤ 5 items, the news item `big: true`)
  - `scale` for size comparisons (2–4 circles, area ∝ value — great for "X times bigger/smaller")
  - `facts` for who/what/how much, `counter` for one big number, `chart` only with real data, `quote` for one short attributed quote
  - `map` with `marker`/`arc`/`tag` when geography matters (launch trajectories, where a study was done, where a regulation applies)
- **End:** globe, two punchlines, sources line.
- Section names like THE PRIZE · THE DETECTOR · THE PAYOFF / THE FINDING · HOW IT WORKS · WHY IT MATTERS. Vary scene mixes day to day.

## Upload
- Title ≤ 60 characters + ` #shorts`, factual ("A Detector in Antarctic Ice Just Won a Nobel").
- Description: 2–3 sentences, then `Sources: …` (outlets + the original paper/mission page), then hashtags (`#science` or `#tech` + topic). The desk disclaimer is appended automatically.
- Tags: `science` or `technology`, `news` + topic-specific (mission, institution, company, field).
